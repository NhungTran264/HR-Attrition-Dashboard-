# HR Attrition Analysis

## Project Overview
This project analyzes the IBM HR Analytics Attrition dataset using Power BI to identify key factors driving employee attrition. The goal is to uncover patterns across departments, demographics, compensation, and work conditions to support data-driven HR decision-making.

---

## Tools Used
- Power BI Desktop
- DAX (Data Analysis Expressions)
- Power Query

---

## Key Business Questions
- Which departments and job roles have the highest attrition?
- How do age, gender, and marital status relate to attrition?
- Does income level affect employee turnover?
- How does overtime and business travel impact attrition?
- At what point in an employee's tenure does attrition peak?

---

## Key KPIs
- Total Employees: 1,470
- Attrition Count: 237

- Avg Monthly Income: $6.5K
- Avg Age: 36.92

## Dashboard Features
**Overview**
- Company-wide KPIs (Total Employees, Attrition Count, Attrition Rate, Avg Income, Avg Age)
- Attrition by Department, Gender, and Age Group
- Attrition by Business Travel, OverTime, and Job Satisfaction

**Attrition Analysis**
- Attrition by Job Role and Marital Status
- Attrition trend by Years at Company
- Attrition by Income Group

**Employee Profile**
- Avg Monthly Income, Avg Age, and Avg Years at Company
- Avg Monthly Income by Department
- Total Employees by Age Group and Work-Life Balance Level

Interactive slicers (Department, Job Role) and bookmark-based navigation buttons allow seamless switching between the three dashboard views.

---

## Key Findings
- The **Research & Development** department recorded the highest attrition count (133), followed by Sales (92).
- **Single** employees had the highest attrition share (50.63%) compared to Married (35.44%) and Divorced (13.92%) employees.
- Attrition peaked sharply in an employee's **first year** at the company (59 cases), then dropped significantly from year 2 onward.
- Employees earning **under $3,000/month** had the highest attrition (113), with attrition decreasing steadily as income increased.
- **Laboratory Technician** was the job role with the highest attrition count (62), followed by Sales Executive (57).
- Employees who worked **overtime** had a higher attrition count (127) than those who didn't (110), making overtime one of the strongest attrition indicators in the dataset.
- Employees with **frequent business travel** showed a higher attrition share (29%) compared to non-travel roles (5%), despite "Travel Rarely" making up the largest share overall.
- The majority of employees rated their **Work-Life Balance as "Better"** (893 employees), indicating generally healthy working conditions across the company.

## Project Outcome
This dashboard identifies the key drivers of employee attrition — including income level, job role, tenure, and overtime — providing HR teams with actionable insights to design targeted retention strategies for high-risk employee segments.
